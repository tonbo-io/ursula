## Durability

An append is acknowledged once a **majority of voters** has replicated it. A single node failure cannot lose an acknowledged write. Acknowledged data lives in replicated hot state across the cluster, and a background worker flushes older segments to S3 on a configurable interval, after which the data inherits S3-grade durability.

In a cross-region deployment with a 5-second flush interval, per-message durability is approximately 9-10 nines. The window where acknowledged-but-unflushed data is at risk from a simultaneous multi-region failure is measured in seconds.

## Consistency

Writes are **linearizable**. Each Raft group serializes appends through its current leader, which assigns a total order.

Catch-up reads may be served by any replica that has applied the relevant stream state. Followers can return already replicated historical bytes locally because stream positions are immutable. Tail-sensitive reads still preserve protocol-visible semantics: `HEAD`, `Stream-Up-To-Date: true`, `Stream-Closed: true`, and `offset=now` are generated by the leader path unless the follower has applied a terminal closed state. Requests that need a write-side access transition, such as expiry or TTL touch, are also routed to the leader.

A catch-up client recovering from an acknowledged append can request
`consistency=leader`. That opt-in routes the ordinary GET through the owning
Raft leader instead of treating a follower-local tail as authoritative. The
leader first confirms with a majority of voters that it still leads (Raft
ReadIndex) and waits until it has applied everything committed up to that
point, so the read is **linearizable**: it reflects every append acknowledged
before the read started, including appends acknowledged by a newer leader. A
leader cut off from the majority redirects to the new leader or answers `503`
with `Retry-After` instead of serving its stale state. The confirmation costs
one round trip to the voters; concurrent leader reads to the same Raft group
share rounds, and a read waits for its confirmation before it reaches the group,
so appends and other reads to the group do not queue behind it. A leader that misses a confirmation under
load retries it for up to one election timeout before it answers `503`, so a
client should retry a `503` after `Retry-After`. It is not a live-read mode; long-poll
and SSE already have leader-owned watcher semantics. The [Read path](/docs/specs/extensions#92-leader-consistency)
section of the extensions specification states this contract normatively.

`HEAD`, `GET …/bootstrap`, snapshot reads and the registration of a long-poll
or SSE reader take the same leadership confirmation on the leader, as do
`offset=now`, `record=` and `tail_records=` on a `consistency=leader` read.
Reads that promise no linearizability skip it and use the leader's applied
state: `offset=now`, `record=` and `tail_records=` without
`consistency=leader`, the start of an SSE session, and a long-poll's timeout
answer.

With `consistency=leader`, `Stream-Up-To-Date: true` on a read means it reached the committed tail. On a local read it means the tail as the serving node knew it: a leader that has lost leadership without knowing it yet can mark a stale tail. A response without it is partial; keep reading from `Stream-Next-Offset`.

## Availability and durability (standard layout)

Three voting replicas across availability zones, plus two non-voting replicas in a second region.

| Property | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Write availability** | Writes continue as long as a majority of voters is healthy. The layout tolerates any single voting-AZ failure. Non-voting replicas hold extra copies but do not vote. |
| **Read availability** | Any reachable replica can serve replicated historical catch-up data locally. Reads that need fresh tail metadata, leader-owned state changes, or live watcher ownership still track write availability per group, not per cluster. |
| **Per-message durability** | ~9-10 nines with a 5-second flush interval. |
| **Annual zero-loss probability** | ~3-4 nines. Probability of no data-loss events in a year. |
